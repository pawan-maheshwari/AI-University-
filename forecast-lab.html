How accuracy is checked

A forecast is only useful if you know how wrong it is likely to be. The lab never grades a model on data it learned from. It checks accuracy five ways, then rolls them into one confidence score with every part shown.

1. Split by time, never at random

Shuffling rows would let a model peek at the future. The lab cuts the history in order. Drag the slider to see how the parts move.

70%

Training: models learn here. Validation: each model forecasts it blind, and the lowest error wins. Test: scored once with the winner, after it is chosen, so nothing was tuned to it. Forecast: the winner is refitted on everything and looks ahead.

2. Always beat a simple baseline

"Same period last season" (seasonal naive) is the bar every model must clear. Skill is how much lower the model's test error is than the baseline's. A clever model that cannot beat the baseline should not be used, and the lab says so.

3. Backtest across several past periods

One test window can be lucky. A rolling-origin backtest pretends to stand at several past dates, forecasts ahead from each, and records the error. Steady errors across folds mean the method is dependable. Step through the folds:

4. Ranges that are tested, not assumed

The forecast range comes from the model's real errors on validation (split conformal prediction), so it makes no assumptions about the shape of the data. The lab then counts how many test actuals landed inside it. If an 80% range catches 80% of test actuals, the range is honest.

5. Keep watching after go-live

Upload new actuals as they arrive. The tracking signal (running total of errors ÷ average absolute error) flags a forecast that keeps missing in one direction; beyond ±4 is a warning. If error rises well above what the test promised, the lab recommends retraining.

The measures

MeasureWhat it meansGood looks like
WAPETotal absolute error ÷ total actual units. Big items count more than tiny ones, which matches the money at stake.Lower is better. Under 20% is strong for weekly retail at product level.
BiasTotal forecast minus total actual, as a share of actual. Shows a habit of over- or under-forecasting.Close to 0%. Persistent negative bias leads to stock-outs; positive to markdowns.
MASEError relative to the naive method's error on the training data.Below 1 means better than naive.
Skill1 − model WAPE ÷ baseline WAPE on test.Above 0 means the model earns its place; 30% or more is excellent.
CoverageShare of actuals inside the stated range.Close to the range you chose (80%, 90% or 95%).
Tracking signalRunning sum of errors ÷ mean absolute error, for each series after go-live.Between −4 and +4.

The confidence score

Six checks, weighted and added to a score out of 100: beats the baseline (30), accuracy on test (20), honest ranges (15), stable backtest (15), validation and test agree (10), enough history (10). 75 or more is High, 50 to 74 Medium, below 50 Low. Each check is shown with your result, so the score is never a black box.

Kidswear margin reset: a proof of concept

A retail fashion business loses margin in Kidswear through overbuying, the wrong size mix, blunt clearance markdowns and quality problems. This proof of concept tested four machine-learning fixes on two seasons of realistic sample data, scoring each against today's rule of thumb on a held-out winter clearance.

What was built

QuestionMethod
Forecast true demandLightGBM with a Tweedie loss, sales lifted back to demand where sizes sold out, 16-trial tuning on validation weeks, P10–P90 ranges, SHAP explanations.
Send the right sizesSize curves learned for each store cluster from sizes that did not sell out.
Clear stock for more profitA size-aware markdown optimiser using the forecast's price response, constrained to clear the same share of stock.
Find quality gapsA text model that reads return comments, then ranks lines by quality-driven returns.

The Python pipeline behind it trains on 898,560 weekly rows. Forecast Lab brings the same discipline (time-based splits, a baseline to beat, a test scored once, tested ranges) to any file you upload. The Kidswear sample in the lab is the same data, summed to category and store cluster.
