Course overview

Why this course exists

Machines now draft, summarise and polish on demand. What they cannot do is read a room that has gone quiet, hold a position under pressure, or make a person feel understood. As the cost of producing words falls to nearly nothing, the premium shifts to judgment, presence and trust — and all three travel through communication.

Yet almost nobody is taught this deliberately. We learn our craft, then improvise the part where we explain it. The result is familiar: a strong recommendation buried on slide 34, a status update nobody finishes, feedback delayed until it becomes a complaint, an idea lost to whoever said it more clearly.

This course treats communication as a trainable motor skill, not a personality trait. You get a small number of frameworks that survive pressure — each one rendered as something you can push, drag, break and rebuild — then you apply them to your own meetings, mails, and the difficult conversation you have been postponing since March.

How it is designed. Solo learning usually dies of two causes: no feedback and no forcing function. Both are engineered out here.

  • Concepts are operable. Every framework has a live diagram. You change an input and watch the outcome move.
  • Labs score you. Sorting, sequencing, rewriting and building exercises tell you immediately whether you understood it.
  • Trainers measure you. Words per minute, pause length, filler count, cut percentage — numbers, not impressions.
  • Three named witnesses. Recruit three people who agree to give blunt feedback at week four and week eight.
  • One public moment. The capstone is delivered to real humans. A deadline with an audience does what willpower cannot.
The weekly loop

How each module runs

Same five steps every week. The middle three happen on this page.

Step 1

See it

An interactive diagram makes the concept visible. Break it deliberately to learn what failure looks like.

Step 2

Test it

Sort, sequence and choose. Immediate feedback tells you whether you have the distinction or only the vocabulary.

Step 3

Build it

Feed in your own situation; the builder returns a script or brief you can copy and use today.

Step 4

Do it live

One field assignment at work. Recorded whenever it involves speaking, because memory flatters you.

A note on the trainers

The pace, pause and filler trainers use your own timing, tapped on the button — no microphone, no upload, nothing leaves the device. Record separately on your phone when a drill asks you to review yourself.

Capstone

The showcase

A course taken alone still needs a moment where it counts. In week eight you deliver, to real people, in real conditions.

Part 1 — The five-minute talk

To a live audience of at least five: a team meeting, a community group, a meetup. It opens with a story, carries one governing message with three supports, and closes with a specific ask. No slides.

Part 2 — The one-page brief

The same argument in writing, using the decision-brief builder from Module 06. Send it to someone who can act on it.

Part 3 — The 360

Your three witnesses answer in writing: What was my point? What did you feel? What would you now do? What one habit should I change?

Part 4 — Retake the diagnostic

Same twenty-four statements, eight weeks apart. Compare the two shapes and write a one-page plan for the next quarter.

Pass standard

You have completed the course when

  • All three witnesses can state your point, and it matches your intent.
  • You score 3 or better on every rubric dimension, against a recording.
  • Your filler count in week eight is materially lower than week one.
  • The overdue conversation from Module 07 has actually happened.
  • Your story bank holds eight stories, three told aloud to a real audience.

Who should enrol

For people whose ideas deserve better transmission

  • Technical specialists asked to “present this to leadership” who dread it
  • Managers stepping into roles where influence replaces authority
  • Delivery and programme leads aligning stakeholders with competing incentives
  • Consultants and pre-sales professionals who live or die on the first three minutes
  • Founders about to explain the same idea a hundred times
  • Experienced professionals who present competently but rarely memorably
  • Anyone told they are “too detailed”, “hard to follow” or “too quiet in the room”
Honest caveats

Who will struggle with it

There is no instructor, no cohort and no register. If you will not record yourself, will not ask three people for blunt feedback, and will not run the field assignments at work, the labs alone will not carry you.

It also does not treat a clinical fear of public speaking, a speech disorder, or communication in a language you are still acquiring. Those need specialist help, and getting it is not a detour from this work.

One more: if your organisation punishes candour, Module 07 carries real risk. Use the frameworks to be precise rather than to be brave.
