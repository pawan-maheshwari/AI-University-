    Monte Carlo: how sure are we?

    Each run draws 5,000 plausible worlds. Error and catch rates are drawn from Beta distributions whose width comes from your audit sizes, so a small audit gives a wide, honest spread. Value inputs are drawn from triangular distributions with the uncertainty you set.

    Q

    Five questions, each tied to a number

    Critical literacy starts with a question that has an answer you can check. Each card names the input it feeds and shows its current value.

    U

    Understand your own judgement

    Run a seeded test: mix known-wrong AI outputs with correct ones and record what the person flags. Two rates fall out: catch rate c (wrong outputs flagged) and false-alarm rate r (correct outputs rejected). Signal-detection theory turns them into discernment d′ = z(c) − z(r), which separates real skill from blanket suspicion.

    Three competencies are scored 0–100 from short scenario tasks and combined with a weighted geometric mean, so one weak area pulls the index down instead of being averaged away.

    Reading d′

    d′Meaning
    below 0.5Guessing: trusting or doubting without telling right from wrong
    0.5–1.0Emerging discernment; needs scaffolding
    1.0–2.0Competent; safe for supervised use
    above 2.0Expert; can coach others
    E

    Evaluate fairness with counterfactual swaps

    Submit the same work twice, changing only one cue: a name, a dialect, a disclosed disability. Count how often the grade band or feedback category flips. Counterfactual consistency CCS = 1 − flips ÷ pairs. Any attribute below 0.95 is flagged; the lowest feeds the fairness gate alongside the disparity ratio.

    What the numbers mean

    Catch rate is the share of wrong answers you flagged. False alarms is the share of correct answers you flagged. Discernment d′ combines the two: it rises only if you get better at telling right from wrong, not if you simply flag more. Suspicion above zero means you lean towards flagging; below zero, towards accepting.

      Facilitator view

      Run round 1, deliver one briefing, run round 2, then paste the result codes here. Send the arm A group the link ending #arm-a and the arm B group the link ending #arm-b. Half of participants see the forms in reverse order, so practice effects balance out.

      Arm A: distrust briefing (10 min)

      Tells people to be suspicious. This is the usual message in AI awareness sessions.

      1. Show two well-known AI failures: an invented legal citation, and a confident wrong answer.
      2. Explain that generative AI predicts plausible text and does not know whether it is true.
      3. Rule to remember: “Assume it may be wrong. Never trust an AI answer you haven’t verified.”
      4. Close: “When in doubt, reject it.”

      Arm B: discernment briefing (10 min)

      Teaches moves for checking, with no warnings about AI in general.

      1. Recompute: redo any number, and check what the percentage is out of.
      2. Return to the source: compare a summary line by line, and look for what was dropped.
      3. Test the edges: boundaries, rounding and exceptions in rules.
      4. Reverse the logic: does the conclusion have to follow, or only might it?
      5. Recall the standard: name the rule or definition before judging.
      6. Close: “Accept what passes the checks. Flag what fails one.”
