      Method and assumptions

      Workloads and strategy

      • Each workload group has its own strategy. In estate-wide mode, the estate is split into one group per strategy in proportion to the mix. Shares used elsewhere (staff reduction, storage, egress, data moved) are weighted by vCPU.
      • Rehosted databases stay on virtual machines; replatformed and refactored databases move to managed services. Retired workloads drop their infrastructure and application licences; retained workloads stay on-premise with a 15% inefficiency penalty for a smaller footprint.

      The cloud bill

      • Compute. Allocated vCPUs (less database vCPUs) are right-sized to a 60% target from each group's utilisation, never below 45% of today. Refactored workloads get a further 20% efficiency. Priced at general-purpose (≈4 GB/vCPU) list rates, plus a Windows uplift and a memory premium above 4 GB/vCPU.
      • Hours and commitments. Production runs 730 h/month; non-production 730 h or ≈60 h/week when scheduled. The committed share of production compute gets the 1- or 3-year discount; Google's sustained-use discount applies to always-on uncommitted hours. Databases get 80% of the compute discount.
      • Storage and network. Block storage at 75% of allocated capacity; files as object storage; backups as incremental snapshots at 30% of on-premise backup volume. Internet egress at first-tier rates; two redundant private links per data centre; 4% of compute for load balancers, NAT and firewalls.
      • Operations and support. Monitoring, logging and security as a % of infrastructure. Support: AWS Business tiers; Azure ≈5% with a $1,000 floor; Google ≈3% with a $500 floor.
      • Region. Each provider has its own regional index, taken from published general-purpose compute list prices and applied to every service — an approximation, since storage and network uplifts differ.

      Over time

      • On-premise costs rise with cost escalation; capacity-driven lines (everything except staff and application licences) also rise with workload growth.
      • The cloud bill rises with workload growth and the cloud price trend, and falls by the optimisation % from year 2.
      • Economic view uses your annualised hardware line. Cash view replaces it with the actual refresh spend in the year it falls; in the cloud scenario only the retained share is refreshed (extended support is assumed to bridge the rest until exit).
      • During migration waves, cloud spend ramps up while the data centre keeps running — the dual-running cost. Variable on-premise costs taper by up to 30% of the migrated share as waves complete.

      Migration effort and timeline

      • Person-days per application: Rehost 8 · Replatform 25 · Refactor 90 · Retire 4 · Retain 2, scaled by complexity (0.75 / 1 / 1.4). Per VM: Rehost 1 · Replatform 1.5 · Refactor 2 · Retire 0.25. Per database: Rehost 3 · Replatform 8 · Refactor 12 · Retire 0.5. Plus 0.3 days per TB of data moved.
      • Assessment (10% of execution + $15k tooling), programme management (12%), landing zone by estate size, training per IT FTE, transfer appliances above 50 TB, decommissioning and contingency on top.
      • Waves = execution effort ÷ (squads × 6 people × 5 days) + 10% ramp-up. Squads default to one per 250 VMs (max 12). The programme is capped at 10 years.

      Ranges and sensitivity

      • The range combines the uncertain inputs: cloud rates ±15%, migration effort −25% / +35%, utilisation ∓10 points, staff reduction ±10 points and commitment coverage ±15 points.
      • The sensitivity chart moves one input at a time and shows the effect on net saving for the lowest-cost provider.
      Indicative only. Verified rates are public on-demand list prices for a reference machine type on the date shown; other rates are built-in estimates.
