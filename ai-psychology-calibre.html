AI has a psychology stream. It studies the wrong half.

We are building tools to read the machine's character. For the person using it, we have only fragments.

AI's progress has mostly been told as a story about capability: bigger models, better benchmarks, more autonomy. Over the last few years a second story has started. Researchers now study models the way psychologists study people, running experiments on their reasoning, biases and personality. They call it machine psychology.

That work matters, but it measures only one party. An AI answer becomes a decision only after a person reads it, trusts it, changes it or ignores it. The same model can make one professional sharper and another more passive. The difference sits in the human: how they trust, how much thinking they keep, how they feel about AI, and whether they notice any of it.

Parts of this are already measured. Validated scales exist for trust in automation, for AI anxiety and for AI literacy. What is missing is one profile that brings them together for an individual and tells them what to do next.

I call that the person's AI psychology. My argument is that it deserves to be a stream of its own: something you can understand, measure and deliberately improve, in the same way we now treat a model's behaviour. My Judgment Imperative framework sets out how organisations should keep oversight of AI. CALIBRE applies the same idea to one person's mind.

How it evolved: two lanes that are now converging

Both lanes started early: one studying how machines behave, the other how people behave around machines. Until recently they ran in parallel. Since 2025 they have been colliding.

Machine side: studying the model's mindHuman side: studying the user's mind

Two sides of one system

Machine psychology and human AI psychology ask mirror-image questions. The most important point is where they meet: model sycophancy does damage when it lands on a person who wants approval and trusts fluent answers. That is why fixing models alone is not enough.

Why now: the evidence is no longer anecdotal

Each study below has limits, and most show correlation rather than cause. Together they point one way: how we use AI is reshaping how we think, and that shaping can be steered.

    What this framework cannot do yet

    A framework that hides its weak points is marketing. Here are CALIBRE's, and how they will be tested.

    Self-report has a blind spot. The people who over-trust AI most may also notice it least, so they may score themselves too kindly. Treat a high score as a claim to check: ask a colleague to rate you on the same statements, or keep a two-week log of how often you verify AI output.

    The statements are new and unvalidated. Four items per dimension is the practical minimum. Some dimensions, especially Agency, Boundaries and Reflection, may overlap. The 45 and 70 cut-offs are judgement calls, and every dimension carries equal weight.

    Trust is two-sided. A middle score can hide strong over-trust balanced by strong avoidance, so the results also show which way your trust leans.

    The evidence base is young. Most studies cited here are correlational or self-reported, and one is an unreviewed preprint with a small sample.

    Validation plan. A pilot with at least 200 professionals; internal consistency for each dimension (Cronbach's alpha of 0.7 or above); a factor analysis to test whether seven dimensions really separate; a test-retest check; and comparison with established scales, such as the Short Trust in Automation Scale, AI anxiety measures and the Meta AI Literacy Scale.

    Where this leads

    My view of how the stream develops. These are forecasts, not findings. The pattern to watch: each step on the machine side creates a matching need on the human side.

    2026

    Self-awareness

    Individuals take self-checks like CALIBRE and start building deliberate habits.

    Machine side: labs measure and steer model traits such as sycophancy.

    2027–28

    Adaptive tools

    AI assistants notice over-reliance and nudge users to think first, check, or take over.

    Machine side: models that read the user's state as well as their prompt.

    2029–30

    Organisational readiness

    Firms track the AI psychology of teams the way they track cyber hygiene, as a condition for giving agents more autonomy.

    Machine side: agents acting with little supervision.

    2030+

    Human alignment

    Calibration of the human becomes the recognised twin of model alignment, taught in schools and professional training.

    Machine side: highly capable systems whose safety depends on people who can still judge them.
