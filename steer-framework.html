Phase twoOutput · Fairness audit & ethical red lines
T

Tension-test

Trade-offs are inevitable — make them explicit, decide them deliberately.

Governance is not about eliminating trade-offs. It is about deciding them in the open — and writing down what you will never trade.

2.1

Bias pipeline analysis

Trace bias entry points across the lifecycle: historical data → labels → features → objective function → thresholds → human use.

Helios found "pin-code" acted as a proxy for religion and caste in Indian portfolios; 1990s repayment data encoded redlining-era patterns into "creditworthiness."
2.2

Fairness tensions & trade-off register

Log each tension (accuracy vs. equity, personalization vs. privacy, speed vs. review), the options considered, the decision, and the owner.

Register entry T-04: equalizing approval rates across protected groups costs 1.8% portfolio accuracy. Decided: accept cost; CRO signed; revisit at annual model review.
2.3

Ethical red lines for your context

Non-negotiables that no business case can override. Few, absolute, board-ratified.

Helios ratified five red lines (below), including "no fully autonomous loan denial without human appeal path" — invoked once in 2026 to block a vendor feature.
2.4

Non-discrimination compliance check

Test outcomes against applicable law: EU AI Act Art. 10 (data governance), ECOA-style fair-lending rules, Indian DPDP Act, local equality statutes.

Quarterly disparate-impact test: approval-rate ratio across gender held at 0.91 (above the 0.80 internal floor); one regional anomaly escalated to the council in Q1.

Interactive — feel the trade-off (Helios credit model)

Drag the threshold. There is no setting where nothing is sacrificed — which is precisely why the decision must be owned, documented, and reviewable.

Helios — ethical red lines (board-ratified)

1

No fully autonomous adverse decision. Every loan denial, account closure, or fraud freeze has a human appeal path with a 10-day SLA.

2

No protected attributes or close proxies as features. Proxy audit (pin-code, name, device language) runs at every retrain.

3

No customer data into external models without contractual no-training guarantees and regional data residency.

4

No deployment of an undocumented model. No model card, no production — regardless of revenue at stake.

5

No dark patterns from the GenAI assistant. It must disclose it is AI, must not impersonate staff, and must hand off on request.

Phase threeOutput · Governance charter & policy framework
E

Establish

Principles without decision rights are poetry — name who decides.

Who decides, who is consulted, who can veto — and what happens when they disagree?

3.1

Governance principles — tensions acknowledged

State each principle alongside the tension it creates, so the charter reads as honest rather than aspirational.

"We pursue innovation velocity AND demand pre-deployment review — when they conflict, risk tier decides: Tier 1 waits, Tier 3 ships with monitoring."
3.2

AI Governance Council — charter & membership

A standing cross-functional body with quorum rules, cadence, veto rights, and a published escalation path to the board.

Helios council: CRO (chair), CDO, CISO, General Counsel, Head of Data Science, business-line lead, employee representative, external ethics advisor. Monthly; quorum 5; CRO veto on Tier 1.
3.3

Roles, decision rights & accountability

A decision-rights table (below) so every consequential call has exactly one accountable name — committees advise, individuals answer.

Tier 1 deployment approval: Accountable = CRO. Model retraining sign-off: Accountable = model owner. Red-line exception: nobody — escalate to board.
3.4

Policy framework & council design

A small policy stack — acceptable use, procurement, data, incident response, model lifecycle — each mapped to an owner and review cycle.

5 policies, each ≤4 pages, owned, reviewed annually. The acceptable-use policy is the only one all 12,000 staff must read; the rest bind specific roles.

Phase fourOutput · Documentation & assurance layer
E

Evidence

Governance is whatever you can prove in 48 hours.

When the regulator, the auditor, or the journalist calls — everything else is intention.

4.1

Model card — applied to your systems

One card per production system: purpose, data lineage, performance by subgroup, known limits, and the human-oversight design. Expand the live template below.

Helios credit model card v3.2: 14 sections, 6 pages, subgroup AUC table, "do not use for" list, owner + reviewer signatures, next-review date on the cover.
4.2

Decision log structure

An append-only register of governance decisions: what, who, when, evidence considered, dissent recorded. Dissent is data, not noise.

Entry DL-2026-014: GenAI assistant scope extension to investment FAQs — approved 6–1; employee representative's dissent (mis-selling risk) logged with mitigations attached.
4.3

Regulatory compliance map

One cross-walk from EU AI Act articles to NIST AI RMF functions to ISO/IEC 42001 clauses — so one piece of evidence serves three frameworks (table below).

Helios's single risk-assessment artifact satisfies EU AI Act Art. 9, NIST MAP/MEASURE, and ISO 42001 §6.1 simultaneously — written once, cited thrice.
4.4

Audit trail & assurance mechanisms

Immutable logs of model versions, inputs to consequential decisions, overrides, and incidents — plus periodic independent review.

Every credit decision stores model version, feature snapshot hash, score, threshold, and reviewer ID for 7 years; internal audit replays a 1% sample quarterly.

Roll out & review

Governance dies two ways: it never ships, or it ships and is never reviewed.

A 90-day plan with named owners prevents the first. A quarterly scorecard prevents the second.

"By 2027, every consequential AI decision at Helios will be explainable to the customer it affects, defensible to the regulator who asks, and reversible by the human who owns it."
